About

I am currently serving as a Temporary Lecturer in the Department of Statistics and Computer Science, Faculty of Science, University of Peradeniya, Sri Lanka. I studied here too, and finished my BSc (Hons) in Computer Science with First Class Honours.

My interests lie in natural language processing and multimodal AI. I'm especially drawn to making large language models and vision-language models more reliable, and useful for everyone, including people who speak low-resource languages. For my undergraduate project, I used text prompts to style moving objects in videos while keeping flicker to a minimum. Right now, I'm studying why vision-language OCR models struggle with Sinhala, and how a script-aware tokenizer can make them faster without losing accuracy.

I teach computer vision and NLP, and run labs for database management systems, parallel programming, and C and Java programming. Outside the classroom, I love graphic design and often volunteer to design for research conferences and academic events.

Research

Published · RESCON 2025With Dr. R. Siyambalapitiya

Temporal-aware, text-driven style transfer for moving objects in video

Most video style transfer methods flicker, because each frame is styled on its own. They also need a reference style image. I built a pipeline that styles only the chosen objects in a video from a plain text prompt, and keeps the result stable from frame to frame.

The pipeline detects objects with YOLOv8, tracks them with DeepSORT, cuts them out with the Segment Anything Model, and styles them with CLIPStyler. The best approach styles the whole frame first, then uses the masks to put only the styled object back onto the original background. I trained and tested it on an elephant dataset I collected and labelled myself.

In progress · 2026Vision-language OCR for Sinhala

Sinhala-aware tokenization for reading scanned and phone-captured documents

Vision-language OCR models write their output in small pieces called tokens. Their tokenizers were built mostly for English, so they break a Sinhala word apart at almost every vowel sign. A Sinhala word costs about eight tokens, against fewer than two for an English word. That makes reading slow, and long pages get cut off at the output limit or drift into the wrong script.

I'm adding a Sinhala-aware vocabulary to LightOnOCR-2-1B and fine-tuning it with LoRA. I'll compare it with the original tokenizer on character error rate, tokens per page, reading time and cut-offs, using scanned Sinhala Acts and my own phone-captured book pages.

What I want to work on next

Research directions I hope to explore during my PhD, in natural language processing, large language models and multimodal AI.

Multilingual and low-resource NLP

Building language models that fairly support many languages and cultures, including low-resource languages like Sinhala.

Multimodal learning

Combining text, images and speech so models can align, ground and reason over information from different sources.

Trustworthy and interpretable AI

Understanding why language models behave the way they do, and detecting and reducing hallucinations and misinformation.

Reasoning in LLMs

Studying the limits of logical and causal reasoning in large language models, and finding ways to make it more robust.

Language agents

LLM-powered agents that can plan, use tools and learn from feedback to complete long, complex tasks.

Knowledge-grounded NLP

Using retrieval-augmented generation, question answering and information extraction to give accurate, evidence-based answers.

Efficient and adaptable models

Small models and efficient fine-tuning that adapt to new languages, domains and tasks with limited data and computing power.

Responsible evaluation

Measuring what language models can really do, where they fail and how robust they are, in situations real users care about.

NLP for social good

Applying language technologies to healthcare, mental health and public services, where reliability matters most.
